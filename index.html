Nanomaterials research

Learning to control matter at the nanoscale

National Taiwan University · 2009–2015 · visiting researcher at Japan’s NIMS

The problem. Nanoparticle size and structure were hard to control reliably, which limited what the materials could do.

The outcome. Widely cited papers, and the experimental-design habits I still use.

What I contributed
Tuned nanomaterials systematically instead of by trial and error

Approach. Applied the Taguchi method to control the particle size and structure of mesoporous silica nanoparticles.

Outcome. My most-cited paper.

Designed catalysts that simplify the process around them

Approach. As a visiting researcher at NIMS, built core-shell catalysts that respond to a magnetic field so they separate easily, and developed a one-pot biodiesel process from algae concentrate with fewer operation units and lower capital cost.

Outcome. Published in ChemSusChem.

Led an international conference program

Approach. Led planning for an international conference as a research assistant, including digital marketing and coordination of the full volunteer team.

Outcome. The conference chair praised the experience delivered to every attendee.

Toolkit

  • Design of experiments (Taguchi)
  • Nanomaterial synthesis
  • Electron microscopy and X-ray diffraction
  • NMR and chromatography

Carried forward: Structured experimentation: change many variables, learn what matters.

Process engineering and sustainability

Redesigning how renewable fuels are separated

Georgia Tech Ph.D. and PSE Fellow · 2015–2020 · advised by Dr. Sankar Nair and Dr. Ryan P. Lively

The problem. Separating renewable fuel molecules took too much energy and money to compete with conventional routes.

The outcome. Process designs that use far less energy, produce fewer emissions and cost less than the alternatives I studied.

What I contributed
Modeled complex adsorption behavior

Approach. Built thermodynamic models with nonlinear regression for multicomponent, non-ideal adsorption of biorefinery molecules in MOF and zeolite adsorbents, and linked molecular behavior to column- and process-scale performance through diffusion and isotherm modeling.

Outcome. Gave process design decisions a quantitative basis.

Simulated and optimized separation processes

Approach. Simulated moving-bed (SMB) systems in AMPL, MATLAB and Python, testing zone configurations, operating conditions and adsorbent and desorbent choices against mass balance and economics.

Outcome. Optimized systems used markedly less energy, produced fewer emissions and cost less than the alternatives studied.

Compared process routes on cost and sustainability

Approach. Ran techno-economic and sustainability evaluations in Aspen HYSYS and Python to compare adsorption with distillation, and simulated pressure-swing distillation with process intensification.

Outcome. Quantified the trade-offs to choose the best configuration, and intensification lowered both capital and energy cost.

Applied early machine learning

Approach. Used machine-learning methods to predict adsorption isotherms and fit model parameters.

Outcome. A first step from physics-based models toward data-driven ones.

Solved industrial problems as a consultant (Kimberly-Clark, 2019)

Approach. Built semi-empirical models linking pulping parameters to product properties, used designed experiments to find what drove chemical retention and product strength, and automated data collection with a rapid offline chemical-quantification method.

Outcome. Shortened development cycles significantly, and a chemical recovery system I built sharply cut feedstock use.

Communicated the work to industry and academic audiences

Approach. Presented at the AIChE Annual Meeting, the Gordon Research Conference and the Frontiers in Biorefining Conference.

Outcome. Poster awards at the Renewable Bioproducts Institute annual conference (2017) and Frontiers in Biorefining (2019).

Toolkit

  • AMPL, MATLAB and Python
  • Aspen HYSYS, gPROMS and pyIAST
  • Nonlinear regression
  • Isotherm and diffusion modeling
  • Simulated moving-bed design

Carried forward: Turning models into decisions about cost and sustainability.

Utility AI

Making grid analytics something crews can act on

Southern Company · Staff Data Scientist · 2025–now · Atlanta

The problem. Insights from millions of smart meters weren’t reliable enough for field crews to act on, and the pipelines behind them were slow.

The outcome. Insights became dependable enough for crews to act on, and pipelines became several times faster.

What I contributed
Made AI insights actionable for field crews

Approach. Redesigned confidence scoring around the decisions crews make before a truck roll, improved interpretability, wrote code changes and directed UI/UX improvements for operational users.

Outcome. The success rate of customer-to-grid insights rose sharply, from unreliable to dependable.

Made a key pipeline several times faster

Approach. Profiled skewed distributed workloads and optimized PySpark partitioning.

Outcome. Runtime dropped to a fraction of what it was, making scheduled runs far cheaper.

Made smart-meter time series trustworthy

Approach. Built time-series imputation and data-quality evaluation for 15-minute and hourly measurements, comparing methods across gap lengths, usage patterns, error, bias and deployment constraints.

Outcome. A practical basis for choosing which method to deploy where.

Surfaced grid problems from sensor signals

Approach. Built voltage-anomaly and grid-observability analytics using normalization, clustering, time-series feature engineering, change-point detection and asset and geographic context.

Outcome. Conditions that deserve engineering review, found automatically.

Built the MLOps foundation and standards

Approach. Designed reproducible Python, PySpark and MLflow workflows on Databricks, Delta Lake and Azure, and authored MLOps standards.

Outcome. Standards submitted for potential reuse by other groups.

Shaped AI governance and edge intelligence

Approach. Works with the AI Center of Excellence on model standards, governance and responsible-AI practices, and guides technical development of edge-based EV and solar detection and local grid coordination.

Outcome. Trusted AI practices at enterprise scale.

Toolkit

  • Python, SQL and PySpark
  • Databricks, Delta Lake and MLflow
  • Azure
  • Time-series imputation
  • Change-point detection

Carried forward: AI strategy, governance and trust at enterprise scale.
